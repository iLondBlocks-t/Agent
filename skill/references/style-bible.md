# Style bible

The target look is flat 2D, warm and restrained, with smooth animation. Think of a short film drawn with simple shapes and lit with care.

## Look
- Palette: purple to coral to amber dusk sky, teal accents, warm windows, rim light, vignette, fine grain. Use the colours in `engine/`.
- Realistic and restrained, never flashy. No decorative circles: no bokeh, no string bulbs, no large dust specks, no sparkle fields, no glowing balls.
- Depth comes from blur per layer, vignette, soft light shafts and wet-floor reflections. Reflections are long soft smears, never discs.
- Hero: round cream face, coral hoodie. Friend: black disc head with white marks, mustard hoodie. Others are in `engine/chars.js`. Keep characters identical from shot to shot: the rig's fixed proportions exist for that.

## Hands
- Simple hands only. Never white mitten hands.
- An arm doing nothing just ends, with no hand drawn.
- A hand holding something is a slim capsule or small fist with thin finger strokes.
- Avoid raised-hand poses unless planned and checked on stills.

## Motion
- Smooth, always moving. No frozen frames. Every scene has breathing, blinks, drift or a slow camera move.
- Walks use planted feet. Feet never slide.
- When walking, elbows trail and forearms lead, the same rule as knees mirrored.
- Prop contact has three beats: grip, carry, release. The hand must reach at every frame, and it leaves in a motivated direction. Never let a hand float next to a prop.
- Hand-offs: both people hold the same object at different points. The second hand closes first, weight dips, the first hand releases and leaves downward, then the new owner draws the object in. Give it an insert shot.
- Doors open first, then the character walks in. A door never passes through anyone. Hinge it on the side away from the path.
- Drinking: show the cup clearly, tilted toward the mouth with the drink visible, ideally with a hand around it.

## Scene coherence
Characters keep the same positions and distances between shots. If someone stands a set distance from a table in one shot, they are that distance in the next.

Build one world with fixed positions, drive everyone from one timeline, and make each shot only a camera on that world. Keep screen direction fixed. Include a wide shot that shows the layout.

## Story feel
Film, not slideshow. Use camera moves, focus, lighting and varied shot lengths. The viewer has switched their brain off to watch, so what the video is about must be obvious straight away and easy to follow.

## Sound
It must match the mood. No uplifting sounds or bright bells in tense scenes. See `sound.md`.
